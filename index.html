How HeatWatch works

Step 1

Measure the room

A small sensor reads temperature and humidity every second and works out how hot it really feels.

Step 2

Watch day and night

The cloud checks every reading, even with the laptop closed, and notices if the sensor goes quiet.

Step 3

Care, together

Family gets phone alerts, and the senior's tablet speaks simple reminders with big I'm OK and Help buttons.

        Warning signs to watch for
        
          
        
            
        Heat exhaustion: cool them down
        
            
          
              
        • Heavy sweating, pale or clammy skin
          • 
              
        • Dizziness, headache, nausea, weakness
          • 
              
        • Fast breathing or heartbeat, extreme thirst, dark urine
          • 
            
        
            
        Move to a cooler spot, give water, apply cool wet cloths, and rest. In BC, call 8-1-1 for nurse advice.
        
          
        
          
        
            
        Heat stroke: call 911
        
            
          
              
        • Confusion, slurred speech, fainting or not making sense
          • 
              
        • Very hot skin, or sweating has stopped
          • 
              
        • Loss of coordination or seizure
          • 
            
        
            
        While waiting, move them somewhere cool and cool the body with water, wet towels or ice packs on the neck, armpits and groin.
        
          
        
          
        
            
        Too cold: warm them up
        
            
          
              
        • Shivering, cold or pale skin, numb fingers
          • 
              
        • Slow or slurred speech, confusion, unusual sleepiness
          • 
              
        • Shivering that stops while they are still cold can mean hypothermia: call 911
          • 
            
        
            
        Warm the room, add dry layers and a blanket, and offer warm drinks and food. Avoid hot baths or heating pads straight on the skin.
        
          
        
          
        Older adults often feel less thirsty and feel the cold less sharply. Offer water and warm drinks regularly even if they say they are fine.
        
        
        
      
        
    
        

    
    
        
      
        
        
        
          
        Alert levels
        
          
        
            
        
            
        
            
        BC health guidance: keep homes below 26 °C
        
            
        
            
        Above 31 °C indoors is dangerous for people at risk
        
            
        UK health guidance: keep homes at 18 °C or warmer
        
            
        
            
        
            
        Pop-up alerts when the level rises
        
          
        
          
        
            
        Levels use the higher of room temperature and feels-like temperature, averaged over the last minute. A level only drops once readings fall 1 °C below its threshold, so alerts don't flicker on and off.
        
            
        The DHT11 sensor is accurate to about ±2 °C. Place it at sitting height, away from windows, lamps, and the Arduino itself. HeatWatch supports, and does not replace, regular in-person checks.
