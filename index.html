      Warning signs to watch for
      
      
      
        
      Heat exhaustion: cool them down
      
        
        
          
      • Heavy sweating, pale or clammy skin
        • 
          
      • Dizziness, headache, nausea, weakness
        • 
          
      • Fast breathing or heartbeat, extreme thirst, dark urine
        • 
        
      
        
      Move to a cooler spot, give water, apply cool wet cloths, and rest. In BC, call 8-1-1 for nurse advice.
      
      
      
      
      
        
      Heat stroke: call 911
      
        
        
          
      • Confusion, slurred speech, fainting or not making sense
        • 
          
      • Very hot skin, or sweating has stopped
        • 
          
      • Loss of coordination or seizure
        • 
        
      
        
      While waiting, move them somewhere cool and cool the body with water, wet towels or ice packs on the neck, armpits and groin.
      
      
      
      
      Older adults often feel less thirsty and may take medicines that raise heat risk. Offer water regularly even if they say they are fine.
      
    
      
  
      

  
      
    
      Settings and data
      
    
      
      
      
      
      BC health guidance: keep homes below 26 °C
      
      
      
      
      Above 31 °C indoors is dangerous for people at risk
      
      
      
      
      Pop-up alerts when the level rises
      
    
      
    
      
      
      
      
    
      
  
      

  
      
    
      Levels use the higher of room temperature and feels-like temperature, averaged over the last minute. A level only drops once readings fall 1 °C below its threshold, so alerts don't flicker on and off.
      
    
      The DHT11 sensor is accurate to about ±2 °C. Place it at sitting height, away from windows, lamps, and the Arduino itself. This tool supports, and does not replace, regular in-person checks.
